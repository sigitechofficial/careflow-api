# DRAFT: Mandatory training non-completion policy

> **Status: draft for HR / registered manager review. Not approved. Not legal advice.**
> Written 16 Nov 2026 from Acas and CQC guidance (sources at the end). Your HR adviser should check it against your contracts, disciplinary policy and capability policy before it is published in CareFlow → Policies.
> After publishing, link it in Policies to **Training**, scope **Section**, section key **`overdue-actions`** so it shows in Learning → Assignments → Review.

## 1. Purpose
To make sure every member of care staff completes the training they need to do their job safely, as required by Regulation 18 of the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014, and to set out a fair, consistent way of handling training that is not completed by its due date.

## 2. Scope
All employed care staff who are assigned mandatory training in CareFlow (online, classroom, virtual, blended or practical).

## 3. Responsibilities
- **Care staff**: complete assigned training by the due date; tell the office early if something stops them (sickness, leave, phone or internet problems, needing help with the app or with reading/language).
- **Managers / training lead**: give reasonable time and support to complete training; review overdue training promptly; record the reason and the action taken in CareFlow.
- **Registered manager**: makes sure this policy is followed and that training gaps are acted on quickly, as CQC expects.

## 4. What happens when training becomes overdue
1. On the night after the due date the course becomes **Overdue** in CareFlow. The carer sees a warning in the app but can still finish the course.
2. A course finished after the due date is recorded as **completed late** and the manager is notified.
3. The manager reviews the overdue course and **finds out the reason first** (Acas: understand why before deciding what to do; consider health and disability, and reasonable adjustments).
4. The manager then either:
   - **extends the due date** (the course opens again), recording why; or
   - **records the reason and the action**; or
   - **marks the course not completed**, records the reason and the action, and may re-assign the course with a new due date.

## 5. Support first
Most overdue training is a capability or support matter, not misconduct. Before any formal step, managers should consider:
- an informal conversation, recorded (an informal conversation is never by itself a formal warning);
- protected/paid time to complete training, extra support, help using the app, learning or language support;
- a supervision 1:1;
- re-assigning the course with a realistic new due date;
- a performance (training) improvement plan with clear objectives, a timescale and the support given.

## 6. Keeping people safe
Where a missing course affects safe care (for example medication, moving and handling, safeguarding), the manager may **restrict duties until the training is complete** (for example no medication visits). This is a safety measure, not a sanction, and is recorded with the review.

## 7. Formal action
Formal action is only taken where support has not worked or there is a conduct issue (for example repeated, unexplained refusal to complete required training). It follows the organisation's **disciplinary / capability procedure** and the Acas Code of Practice, including:
- a formal meeting, with the right to be accompanied;
- a decision in writing, stating the problem, the improvement needed, the timescale and how long any warning lasts;
- a right of appeal.

Possible outcomes, depending on the procedure: first written warning; final written warning; dismissal as a last resort. CareFlow only **records** the decision and links it to this policy version; letters, meetings and appeals follow the HR procedure.

## 8. Records
Every extension, reason, action and outcome is recorded in CareFlow with the date, who recorded it and the version of this policy that applied. Records are kept in line with the organisation's records retention policy.

## 9. Review
This policy is reviewed every year, or sooner if guidance or law changes.
